    The Mending Rooms

    A five-chapter story region where each repair is a workshop arrangement to diagnose, with the three solutions explained.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. How it opens
    2. The five chapters
      1. 1. Things brought in
      2. 2. The clock that ran hot
      3. 3. The lens that would not settle
      4. 4. The music box that forgot
      5. 5. Nothing is only broken

    Warning: This page gives away the three repair puzzles of a mid-game story region, and the plot around them.

    The Mending Rooms are behind the Glass Orchard, at the end of a lane too narrow for a cart. Halda keeps them, and has mended things since before the second stopped. Every shelf holds something a person gave up on, each with a paper tag. Some tags say only "it used to work."

    "People think mending is knowing the answer. It's mostly listening until the thing tells you where it hurts."

    How it opens

    The Mending Rooms open when you expand to the Orbital ring, or record eight discoveries, whichever comes first. The chapters then arrive in order, and each repair only starts counting once you have recorded the chapter before it in your journal. Unlike the Overwound Quarter, these goals are arrangements of your workshop. The game notices the moment you build one. Once a repair is done it stays done, and Folding does not undo it.

    The five chapters

    1. Things brought in

    Halda pushes three tags across the bench: "Your workshop can hear things mine can't. Tell me what these three need. No hurry." There is no goal here, only the invitation.

    2. The clock that ran hot

    Tag: "Carriage clock. Runs hot, loses the afternoon. Wanted back by Thursday."

    Repair: build a board of six or more machines on your first board, including an Escapement, with Load at 80% of capacity or less.

    The clock was asked to do everything at once. Give your board an Escapement and keep it from straining, and it settles into a tick so even that Halda closes her eyes to hear it.

    Tip: Load is the number on the chip above the ring. Aim to sit comfortably under it. See load and stability.

    3. The lens that would not settle

    Tag: "Reading spectacles. Everything swims."

    Repair: route a Lens into a Splitter that has reached level 25 or higher.

    A lens on its own does nothing. It has to be aimed at something steady enough to be worth magnifying. Halda's verdict: "Half of what comes in here isn't broken. It's pointed slightly wrong, and somebody has been blaming it for years."

    4. The music box that forgot

    Tag, in a child's handwriting: "Music box. It forgot the end of the song."

    Repair: have a Reservoir that is holding stored Moments, and an Archive at level 10 or higher.

    The box needs somewhere to keep the tune between one turn and the next. The Reservoir holds it and the Archive writes it down. When the stored Moments release, the music box plays four bars it has not played since the clocks stopped, then plays them again with the ending attached. The child who wrote the tag is in carriage two of Mara's train.

    5. Nothing is only broken

    Halda hangs the three tags on a nail by the door, each one a thing that left under its own power. She gives you a tag of your own: "For when something of yours stops. Write down where it hurts. Then bring it here, or mend it yourself. Either way you will have listened." This last chapter opens once you have recorded the three repairs.

    Quick checklist

    Repair What your workshop needs
    Carriage clock 6+ machines on the first board, one an Escapement, Load at 80% of capacity or less
    Spectacles A Lens routed into a level 25+ Splitter
    Music box A Reservoir with stored Moments, and an Archive at level 10+

    Note: The Lens needs a route to the Splitter, so make sure the arrow points from the Lens to the Splitter. In Forward Time, routes must point forward around the ring, so seat the Lens in an earlier socket. See rings and routes.

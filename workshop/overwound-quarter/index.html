    The Overwound Quarter

    A four-chapter story region built around load. How to open it, the three goals, and what the chapters say.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. How it opens
    2. The four chapters
      1. 1. A district wound too tight
      2. 2. What the mainspring costs
      3. 3. The slower shift
      4. 4. Enough
    3. A thread that carries on

    Warning: This page describes the plot and goals of a mid-game story region.

    The Overwound Quarter is the richest street in Meridia. It opens after your second Fold. Each chapter pairs a story beat with a goal that teaches you something about load. See load and stability.

    How it opens

    Complete two Folds, and the chapter A district wound too tight appears in City and Journal. The remaining chapters open in order. Each goal only starts counting once you have recorded the chapter before it in your journal, so read each one as it arrives.

    Once a goal is met, it stays met as a permanent mark. A chapter never closes again after it opens, and Folding does not undo it.

    The four chapters

    1. A district wound too tight

    Senn returns with his bicycle bell wrapped in a rag. "They don't like sudden noises up there. Everything's wound too tight." When the second stopped, the Quarter's clock-owners found that a held second could still be worked: wind a mainspring a little harder and it gives a little more. So they wound, and kept winding. Aurel Tane, master of the Gilded Works, meets you at the gate with a ledger under his arm. He is proud and exhausted, and asks you why the great spring sounds like that.

    There is no goal for this chapter. It opens the next one.

    2. What the mainspring costs

    Goal: run your board over capacity for one minute in total. You show Aurel by letting your own board run past what it can bear. Nothing explodes, and the machines each give a little less. The journal tracks the time as x:xx / 1:00 over capacity.

    Tip: This goal needs you to overload on purpose. Add machines until Load goes above capacity, wait out the minute, then fix it. The time does not need to be continuous.

    3. The slower shift

    Goal: run eight or more machines at full efficiency for ten minutes. Aurel lets half the springs down by hand. You keep your own board inside its limit and leave it there. If your board goes over capacity, the ten-minute count starts again, and it restarts if you drop below eight machines too.

    Tip: Add an Escapement and a few levels of capacity research, then leave the board alone while you do something else in the game. See load and stability.

    By the end of the shift the Works has made more than it made yesterday. The Counter, listening, says: "I have made the same error. I would like to compare notes."

    4. Enough

    Goal: Fold from a workshop that is not over capacity. When you Fold, the Quarter watches from its windows. Aurel opens the great case and lets the mainspring all the way down, and the note that has hung over the street for years slides lower and is gone. People come out of their doorways.

    He keeps the Works. "I am going to be a long time making this right. I would like to do it at a reasonable pace." On Iona's map, the district gets a new name in pencil. It just says: the Quarter.

    A thread that carries on

    Later chapters of the Mending Rooms return to the apprentices of the Gilded Works. See the Mending Rooms.

    Quick plan

    1. Fold twice and read the first chapter.
    2. Overload your board for one minute, then bring it back under.
    3. Hold eight machines under capacity for ten minutes.
    4. Make sure your board is under capacity at the moment you Fold.

    See also the atlas.

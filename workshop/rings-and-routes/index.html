    Rings, sockets and routes

    How the three rings of sockets work, how to expand them, and the rules for routing one machine into another.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    On this page
    1. The three rings
      1. Costs
    2. Routing
      1. The rules
    3. What routes do
    4. Layouts

    Your workshop is a brass instrument with rings of numbered sockets around a central clock. Machines sit in sockets, and routes carry one machine's output to another.

    The three rings

    Ring Sockets How you get it
    Core Up to 8 Buy sockets one at a time, in order, with Moments.
    Orbital 12 Expand the branch once you can afford it.
    Horizon 16 Expand again after the Orbital ring.

    That is 36 sockets on a single board. Tap Core, Orbital or Horizon to inspect one ring more clearly, and pinch to zoom in when you are planning a layout.

    Costs

    • Core sockets cost, in order: 25, 100, 300, 750, 1,500, 3,000 and 6,000 Moments. They are permanent and survive every Fold.
    • Expanding to the Orbital ring costs 2,500 Moments, and the Horizon ring costs 25,000. Rings reset when you Fold.

    Opening the Orbital ring is one of the early discoveries, and it is how you meet the courier Senn properly. See characters.

    Routing

    To connect machines, tap one, choose Route from here, then tap the destination. The menu hides while you choose, and arrows show the direction.

    The rules

    • Each machine has one output. A Bridge has three.
    • In Forward and Branching Time, a route must point forward: to a later socket clockwise, or out to an outer ring. You cannot route backwards.
    • In Looping Time, routes may go in any direction, including back to where they started.
    • Different rings connect only at their quarter points: the 12, 3, 6 and 9 o'clock sockets. A Bridge removes that limit.
    • In Branching Time, routes stay within one branch. The branches combine through their own bonus. See temporal laws.

    If a route is not allowed, the game tells you why.

    What routes do

    In Forward Time the receiving machine gains +8% output for every Splitter stage feeding into it, up to +40% at five stages. The sender keeps all of its own output, so chaining costs you nothing.

    Other machines use routes differently:

    • A Lens, Synchroniser or Phase Shifter affects the machine it is routed to.
    • A Reservoir or Echo Chamber works on the Splitter routed into it.
    • A Conductor needs no route at all.

    Layouts

    Once a design works, you can save it as a layout, up to twelve of them. After a Fold, apply the layout and the game tells you which machines are missing and what they cost. Layouts load positions, routes, modifications and phases, but not levels, currency or stored production. A layout only applies to the same temporal law and blueprint it was saved under. See folds.

    Tip: Build the chain around the ring clockwise, then place Lenses and Reservoirs on sockets that sit just after your strongest Splitters.

    See also load and stability.

    The story

    Tamsin Hale's notebook, the valley's workshops and the pages that unlock as you stop each Toll.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. The opening
    2. The pages
    3. The valley
    4. The far hills
    5. No ending

    Warning Spoilers for the pages of story unlocked as you play, including all four Tolls.

    The opening

    Every clock in the valley stopped on the same evening. The mills fell still, the workshops went quiet. Only one bell kept ringing.

    It hangs in a small stone tower, kept for years by Tamsin Hale, bell-founder. She is gone now. She left you her notebook, and the key to the belfry.

    Out in the dark, the valley's old wind-ups are waking with Rust in their springs. They are not wicked, only broken, and the last ticking clock is calling them. Raise towers by the roads. Mend every wind-up you stop. And if a minute goes wrong, ring the bell backwards: Tamsin's notebook says you can.

    Wind-ups are stopped and mended, never killed.

    The pages

    The story comes in short pages that open at milestones. They stay in the Foundry's notebook so you can read them again.

    Page Opens when What it says
    The hour's guard you mend your first Warden The Wardens were the valley's finest work, one at every workshop gate, striking the hour as it passed
    Ringing backwards you ring back for the first time "Ring it the wrong way and it remembers the last minute, and lets you have it again. I only know it does, and that it tires."
    One who stayed a wind-up first wakes Some of them always would stay: "Wind them with kindness and a few will wind back"
    Recast your first recast Tamsin never threw a bell away; she melted it down and poured it again a little truer each time
    The toymaker's drummer the first Toll The Drummer led the valley's parade every midsummer. "Bring him home."
    The millstone the second Toll The mill fed the whole valley; its great stone rolled out of the door and the millrace has been still ever since
    Undelivered the third Toll Among the letters the Postmaster carried, one in Tamsin's own hand: "Thank you. Keep going."
    Tamsin's foundry the fourth Toll With the Golem mended you reach her foundry at last, her designs for the great bells pinned to its walls

    The valley

    The workshops that made the wind-ups were a toymaker's, a mill, a post office and a foundry. Each Toll comes from one of them. The legendary towers are Tamsin's own designs, found in her notebook as the story goes on.

    The far hills

    From the foundry's high window you can see, far across the hills, a tall tower with a lamp lit at the top, and beyond it a city standing still in the rain. The keeper doesn't know either place.

    No ending

    The story has no last page. Midnight just comes round again.

    See also The clock face and Wardens and Tolls.

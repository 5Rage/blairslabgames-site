    Towers at a glance

    How towers, rarities, families, limits, levels, stars and final forms fit together in The Last Bell.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    On this page
    1. Rarities
    2. Field limits
    3. How towers unlock
    4. Levels inside a run
    5. Stars, forever
    6. Final forms
    7. Families
    8. Reach

    Towers are built with Parts during a run, and each one has a rarity, a family and its own way of working. This page covers the rules that apply to all of them. The pages for each rarity list every tower.

    Rarities

    Rarity Frame colour Where to read about it
    Common stone grey Common towers
    Uncommon verdigris Uncommon towers
    Rare blue Rare towers
    Epic violet Epic towers (spoilers)
    Legendary gold Legendary, Mythic and Exalted (spoilers)
    Mythic crimson same page
    Exalted pale white-gold same page

    Higher rarities are stronger and cost more Parts, but fewer of them may stand on the field at once. Every tower also has its own shape, so you don't have to rely on colour to tell rarity.

    Field limits

    Rarity At most on the field
    Common and Uncommon as many as you have pads
    Rare 4
    Epic 2
    Legendary 1
    Mythic 1
    Exalted 1

    How towers unlock

    Towers unlock by the best wave you have ever reached, in any run. You begin with the Clapper, Spring-gun and Chime post. Each other tower has its own wave. A few Trials unlock a tower early; see Trials.

    Levels inside a run

    Within a run a tower can be raised to level 10. Each level makes the tower strike harder (damage grows by about 45 percent a level) and reach a little further (about 3 percent), but each upgrade costs about 62 percent more than the last. Selling returns 70 percent.

    Stars, forever

    Separately, each tower has a permanent star rating, bought with Bell-metal in the Foundry. Each star makes that tower strike 25 percent harder in every later run, compounding, up to 15 stars. The first star costs 25 Bell-metal for a Common tower, and more for higher rarities (40, 65, 100, 150, 225 and 300 for Uncommon through Exalted). Each further star costs about 45 percent more than the last.

    Final forms

    At level 10, each tower chooses one of two final forms, each with its own painting. For example, the Spring-gun becomes a Repeater (fires twice as fast) or a Harpoon (each bolt pierces up to three wind-ups in a line). The choice lasts for that tower in that run, so you can choose differently next time. Every form is listed on the rarity pages.

    Families

    Every tower belongs to one of seven families: Bell, Spring, Weight, Air, Light, Oil and Time. Families matter for a few things.

    • Fliers: Spring, Bell and Air towers can generally strike fliers (Gliders); Weight and Oil towers cannot. Each tower's card says so, and so do the pages here.
    • Pairings: some towers reward being used together. See Tower pairings.
    • The moth prefers to settle near Light towers. See The lantern moth.

    Reach

    Reach is measured in tiles of the field, which is 11 tiles across. Fog and some Trials shorten reach for every tower. Lantern posts and similar towers lengthen it for their neighbours.

    Tip One kind of tower won't cover everything. Armour, fliers, healers and umbrellas each need a different counter, and The wind-ups lists them.

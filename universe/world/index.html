    The world

    The Evenwold is the country every one of our games is set in. This page covers its places and how time works there.

    On this page

    The games are set in a country called the Evenwold. It has old roads, hop fields, a wharf, a flooded valley, a moor, a city stopped in time and a hill town at that city's wall. The "even" in the name comes from evenfall, the hour when the lamps are lit and the moths come out, and from keeping time even.

    Nobody in the Evenwold counts years, and none of the games gives a date.

    Time is a material

    In the Evenwold, time can be worked like brass or glass. Clockmakers wind it and keepers hold it. A good workshop can fold it, and a good bell can ring it backwards. The people who do this are tradespeople, such as couriers, keepers, gunners, lamplighters and clockmakers. There are no wizards.

    Places that have stopped

    A lot of the Evenwold has stopped. There was no single disaster, and the places stopped for different reasons.

    • Some places are held. Someone stopped them on purpose and stays to tend them.
    • Some places ran down. One morning nobody came to wind the clock, and nobody has since.

    The Rust gathers where time has been left alone. The games don't explain it further.

    Places you can visit

    Place Game What it is
    Meridia The Last Second Workshop A city held at 23:59:59. The rain has stopped in mid-air and the last train is stuck between stations.
    The great clock tower Hivewright A tower tall enough that its top is in cloud. Its clocks stopped one dawn, and a hive of wind-ups is climbing it and restarting them.
    The valley of stopped clocks The Last Bell A valley where every clock stopped on the same evening, except the bell in one small stone tower.
    Belcourt The Belfry Gun A hill town under a belfry, just outside the edge of Meridia's held second.
    The road to the wall The Lamplighter A long road towards the city wall that you light one lamp at a time.
    The back roads The Roads Between Small towns on old railway lines, in the country between the great clocks.

    Clocks keep places going

    A place in the Evenwold depends on its clocks and bells. Great clocks restart the floors of a tower, bells call the hour, and Meridia's station clock must never move. When a clock stops, the place around it is affected.

    How the games look

    The Last Second Workshop is drawn in navy and brass, Hivewright in amber and verdigris, and The Last Bell in dusk blue and bronze. Lamps, clock faces, letters and small brass mechanisms appear in all of them.

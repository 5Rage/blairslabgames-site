    Chapter 13 plot and boss - The Noon Gnomon

    Corwin faces the Company of Clockmakers, the Hall of Mirrors lets the sun speak, and the Noon Gnomon is finally allowed to move a shadow.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. What happens
    2. Party and moth
    3. Boss: The Noon Gnomon
    4. Hunt boss: Old Sunstruck
    5. What the chapter leaves you with

    Warning This page gives away the plot and the boss of this chapter.

    Place guide: Saltglass. Previous: Chapter 12 plot and bosses. Next: Chapter 14 plot and bosses.

    What happens

    East of Brindle Quay the road turns to white salt, and Saltglass Fair stands in a noon that has not moved in thirty years. Not one shadow shifts. Mayoress Noon welcomes you warmly: Hesper Quill's poster promised that the best of the day need never end, the town took a vote, and even the sundials voted yes. Each Dial-Keeper holds a pole up to the light so no shadow can slip. At the top of the Glass Hill, the Noon Gnomon, built by the Company of Clockmakers a hundred years ago, has told twelve o'clock ever since. Its observatory door is sun-locked, and only the Company's Dial Key opens it.

    Three of the Company are at the Fair: Master Orme, Dunstan Rook and Imelda Frayne. This is Corwin's chapter. The Company put him out of its ranks nine years ago, or so Corwin told his friends at Hollowmere. In the pavilion the full story comes out. Master Orme says he told Corwin to go home as a kindness, and Corwin heard it as a sentence. The Company did nothing when Master Venn built his idea of a clock to stop every clock, and from outside, nothing looked exactly like taking his side. Corwin admits that he took his fear to the Council so someone bigger could say no, and called that courage. Nobody came after him; he never looked back to see. Ottoline gets her own answer: Professor Lisle wrote to the Company for eleven years, and Orme read every letter and had no reply that was not "we were wrong."

    Orme gives Corwin the Dial Key, saying that the Rules allow it to go to any hand a Master would trust with his own bench. His bench by the window has been kept dusted. Dunstan gives him back his old brass loupe. Corwin says he is not coming back to the Company yet, and Orme says the bench stays.

    Up the Glass Hill, the Dial Key opens the observatory. The Gnomon is at the bottom, and a bronze shutter blocks the corridor until the sun speaks. In the east wing, the Hall of Mirrors has three levers and a sign: the sun sees itself in three. Two mirrors lean forward to listen, and one stands back to look. When the beam strikes the dial, the shutter rises.

    The Noon Gnomon is holding the hour and has forgotten how to let go. You fight, and when it falls its pole tilts a hair off true and a long thin shadow slides across the great dial for the first time in thirty years. Down at the Fair, every shadow stirs and stretches away east. The Dial-Keepers lower their poles, Cecily Brine sets off for the forty paces to her sister's tea stall, and Mayoress Noon finds that her shadow has a hat. Orme thanks Corwin, and Corwin promises to try Thursday.

    Party and moth

    • Nobody joins or leaves this chapter. Bonds rise most between Wren and Corwin, and Corwin with Bett and Ottoline.
    • Corwin receives the Dial Key and his old guild loupe.
    • No new moth Light is shown in this chapter.

    Boss: The Noon Gnomon

    A sundial the size of a windmill, with a shadow-pole like a mast.
    HP 36500
    Attack 218
    Defence 45
    Mind 210
    Speed 22
    XP / Marks 8600 / 3600
    Weak to Frost, Tide
    Resists Spark
    Drops Wind Draught (always)

    These are base figures. The game tunes bosses higher, so expect a stiff fight.

    Moves

    • Shadow Pole: a heavy single-target hit.
    • Glare: hits everyone and may Daze.
    • Noon Stands Still: hits everyone lightly and may Seize.
    • Heat Haze: hits one friend and likely Rusts them.
    • Hold the Hour: it hurries itself.
    • High Noon Beam (BIG): hits the whole party hard.
    • The Longest Shadow (BIG): its heaviest single-target hit.

    The moth's Glow shows both BIG moves a turn early. The fixed pattern runs Shadow Pole, Noon Stands Still, Heat Haze, Shadow Pole, Hold the Hour, High Noon Beam, Glare, Shadow Pole, The Longest Shadow, Noon Stands Still, Shadow Pole, Heat Haze, High Noon Beam, The Longest Shadow, then repeats. The first High Noon Beam falls on its sixth move and the first Longest Shadow on its ninth.

    Advice. Bring Frost and Tide (Frost Flasks from Sallow's Sundries work well) and leave Spark at home, since it resists. Guard on Glow turns and keep Oil Cans for Rusting and Seized; a Shade Tea heals everyone at once. If it is too hard, train a few extra fights first, since the Rust returns after a rest.

    Hunt boss: Old Sunstruck

    An optional fight started from the notice board in the Parasol Inn. The clues lead from Rafe Winnow to Madam Pargo and out to the south-east flats. It is looking for shade, and afterwards you put a parasol over it. See Hunts.
    HP 34000
    Attack 330
    Defence 42
    Mind 310
    Speed 26
    XP / Marks 3200 / 950
    Weak to Frost, Tide
    Resists Spark
    Drops Shade Tea (always); the Camel Bell trinket and 400 Marks from the story

    Its BIG moves are Boiler Burst (hits everyone) and Hump Slam (a very heavy single hit); the Glow warns you a turn before each.

    What the chapter leaves you with

    • The Fair Rosette trinket, 2 Shade Tea and 1800 Marks from Mayoress Noon, plus the Arena.
    • A last word from Master Orme: the Company has been very quiet for nine years, and it was not agreement.
    • A pinned notice on the road out points to Candlewick. See Chapter 14 plot and bosses.

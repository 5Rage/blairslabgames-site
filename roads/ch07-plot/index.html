    Chapter 7 plot and boss - Hollowmere

    Corwin tells the truth about the Stillwright, Second Chance is born, and the Keepsake falls away like old wallpaper.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. What happens
    2. Party and moth
    3. Boss: The Keepsake
    4. What the chapter leaves you with

    Warning This page gives away the plot and the boss of this chapter.

    Back to the place guide: Hollowmere. Previous: Chapter 6 plot. Next: Chapter 8 plot.

    What happens

    The road out of the estate comes out above a long still lake with a dam at one end and an old halt on the shore. Wren spots a village under the water. "Hollowmere," says Corwin, and then says nothing for a long time.

    At the dam house, Mr Haverly explains that the valley was flooded for the water thirty years ago and everyone moved out, but the water never got in. The clockmaker, Master Venn, would not leave, and his wife Marta kept the chapel clock. The water rose to the walls and stopped, like glass. Corwin has gone very pale. Mr Haverly says the lake lets you through if you walk the old road.

    The water rises on either side and holds above their heads. The village is dry, held exactly as it was thirty years ago. In held time, Corwin's Rewind does nothing.

    At the post office, one pigeonhole reads "A. VENN, CLOCKMAKER. CHAPEL COTTAGE." It matches the thirty-year-old dead letter in Wren's satchel, addressed "A., Hollowmere". The handwriting matches the name on the chapel clock's plate, "Kept by Marta Venn". The letter is from Marta to her husband, Aldous Venn. Corwin asks everyone to come to the chapel.

    Corwin's confession. Aldous Venn was Corwin's master for eleven years, the finest clockmaker the guild ever had and the kindest man he knew. When the valley flooded, Aldous stopped the village so the water could not have it, and Marta begged him to let it go. She died the next winter. Aldous decided that, since everything that ticks will end, nothing should tick. Nine years ago he came to the guild with plans for the Stilling Engine, a clock to stop every clock on every road at once, and asked Corwin to help build it. Corwin said no and told the guild. The guild took Aldous's side and threw Corwin out.

    The Stillwright, S., is Aldous, and Corwin has known since the windmill. Wren asks why he did not say, and he answers that if he said it aloud it would be true. Bett thanks him for telling them. Ottoline says Aldous is not a monster, just sad, and Corwin calls him the saddest man he ever knew. Wren promises to deliver Marta's letter anyway.

    Party and moth

    • Nobody joins or leaves. The party is Wren, Corwin, Bett and Ottoline.
    • The moth gains no new Light.
    • Corwin's tuning rod rings by itself, and the moth settles on it. He realises the undoing was Aldous's trick all along. Rewind is reforged as Second Chance: twice a battle, it undoes a foe's last move and steadies everyone, and it works in held time too.
    • Corwin's Bond with Wren, Bett and Ottoline grows, and the Venns' cottage unlocks.

    Boss: The Keepsake

    Chapel Cottage is bigger inside than it should be, with a kitchen, a bedroom, a workshop and a wedding, all of a life kept and rusting over. A house of Rust that has grown where nobody lives says "KEEP. HER. KEEP. IT. ALL." Ottoline says it is damp right through and tells Corwin to use Spark.

    These are base figures, and the game tunes bosses higher.
    HP 16000
    Attack 128
    Defence 30
    Mind 120
    Speed 17
    XP and Marks 3200 XP, 1200 Marks
    Weak to Spark
    Resists Frost
    Drops Wind Draught (always)

    Moves in plain words

    • Slam the Door: a plain hit on one hero.
    • Old Wallpaper: a Mind attack on everyone that can leave them Dazed.
    • Hold Still: a light Mind attack on everyone that can leave them Seized.
    • Cold Hearth: a Mind hit on one hero that often leaves them Rusting.
    • Keep: it hurries itself.
    • Every Room at Once (BIG): a heavy hit on the whole party.
    • Remember (BIG): its heaviest hit on a single hero.

    The moth's Glow shows both BIG moves a turn early.

    The pattern. It repeats a fixed fourteen-move cycle: Slam the Door, Old Wallpaper, Cold Hearth, Slam the Door, Keep, Every Room at Once, Hold Still, Slam the Door, Remember, Old Wallpaper, Slam the Door, Cold Hearth, Every Room at Once, Remember.

    Advice. Spark does the most. Use Arc and Storm Jars (80 Spark damage, 60 Marks at the Dam House). Ottoline's Rime and Hard Frost do little. Her Expose makes it take extra damage, and her Read shows what comes next. Save Second Chance, which Corwin can use twice, for the BIG moves or for an Old Wallpaper or Hold Still that would hurt. Use Bett's Bulwark the turn before Every Room at Once and Remember. Keep Oil Cans or Poultice and Thaw ready for Seized and Rusting, and the moth's Wake can shake one friend out of Seized once. Lake Tonic (heals 220 HP) and Rosehip Tonic are your best healing. Its health and Mind are noticeably above the Hothouse, so train a few extra fights first. The Rust returns after a rest.

    What the chapter leaves you with

    After the fight the Rust falls away like old wallpaper, leaving a small cottage with a teapot, two chairs and a wedding photograph. On the desk is an unfinished letter: "Marta. I kept it. Every stone, every cup, the clock you wound on Sundays. You asked me to let it go, and I could not. So I will keep everything else instead. - A." It is signed A. here and S. on all the other notes, in the same hand. Wren holds Marta's letter beside it.

    Bett asks whether they should wind the chapel clock and let the lake in. Wren says no, it is his to wind.

    Corwin says the rail-cart at the halt once ran the branch line to Wicklow, and he can get it going. From Wicklow the line runs on north to the moor, and the rail-cart now carries the party between Hollowmere Halt and Wicklow Halt. Next: The High Moor and the Chapter 8 plot.

    Chapter 1 plot and boss - The Station Clock

    The morning the Wicklow station clock stopped, the first meeting with Corwin, and the fight with the Porter.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. What happens
    2. Party and moth
    3. Boss: The Porter
    4. What the chapter leaves you with

    Warning This page gives away the plot and the boss of this chapter.

    Place guide: Wicklow Halt and the Old Cutting. Next: Chapter 2 plot and boss.

    What happens

    In forty years the 6:12 to Tollbridge has never been late. This morning it did not come at all. Wren notices that Aunt Agnes's kettle has gone quiet, and when she turns round Agnes is standing at the counter with her stamp raised, warm but perfectly still. A small moth made of soft light taps at the window, flies off towards the shop and back, and waits until Wren follows. It gives her the first of its Lights, Glow.

    Outside, everyone who was in the open is frozen where they stood, and the station clock has stopped at twelve minutes past six. By the platform Wren meets a nervous clockmaker called Corwin, who fell asleep in the waiting room after the last train. He explains that anyone out under the clock's face when it stopped stopped with it, and that somebody has pulled the escapement (the part that lets a clock tick) clean out. The moth settles on the clock and then flies a slow loop to the east. Corwin spots rust-marked scrape marks along the platform heading the same way, towards the Old Cutting, and he can't leave a broken clock alone. He joins Wren.

    The two follow the road east and climb into the disused tunnel, where the moth's Glow reveals a hidden way through the wall. At the far end stands the station's old luggage porter, clockwork and rusted through, with a trunk under each arm. It demands tickets, and Wren has to fight it. When it is beaten it sets its trunks down gently, as if putting them on a rack, and Corwin tells it to rest. Wren gets the Station Escapement back.

    Back at the Halt, Corwin fits it and the moth circles the clock face once. The pendulum swings and the village starts again: a loaf lands in a basket, a broom finishes its sweep, a kettle boils somewhere. Old Jory the stationmaster is baffled that it is nearly half past nine.

    Tucked behind the clock face is an envelope addressed "To whoever mends the clocks". The note inside asks the finder to stop: everything that ticks will one day end, let them rest, let the world rest. It is signed only "S." Corwin says quietly that whoever wrote it means to stop more clocks. The moth drifts out along the road and waits for them. The oak has come down across the Tollbridge road, so Corwin suggests the old path north through the Hopfields instead. Wren wants to see Aunt Agnes first, and Agnes sends her off on her first real round with three letters for the Hopfields.

    Party and moth

    • Joins: Corwin (level 2). Nobody leaves.
    • Moth Light: Glow (Light 1), at the start. It lights dark places, shows hidden ways, and in battle shows a BIG move a turn early.
    • Letters: Aunt Agnes gives you the Hopfields Post, three letters to deliver in Chapter 2.

    Boss: The Porter

    The station's old porter, taken by the Rust. It is the gentlest boss in the game, but you only have Wren and Corwin, so don't take it lightly. These are base figures. The game tunes bosses higher, so expect a stiffer fight than the numbers suggest.
    HP 900
    Attack 19
    Defence 9
    Mind 14
    Speed 12
    XP / Marks 120 / 80
    Weak to Spark
    Resists Nothing
    Drops Wind Draught (always)

    Moves

    • Trunk Swing: an ordinary hit on one friend.
    • Check Tickets: a light hit that pushes its target back in the turn order.
    • Heavy Trunk: BIG, one very heavy hit. The moth's Glow warns you a turn before.
    • Rust Whistle: BIG, a magic attack on the whole party with a 50% chance to leave each friend hit Rusting for 3 turns. Glow warns you a turn before.

    Pattern. It repeats the same cycle: Trunk Swing, Check Tickets, Heavy Trunk, Trunk Swing, Rust Whistle, Trunk Swing, Check Tickets, Heavy Trunk, then back to the start. A Heavy Trunk is therefore never far away, and the Whistle lands once in the middle of each cycle.

    Advice. Corwin's Arc is your best damage, and a Spark Jar adds more (buy one at Penhallow's Stores for 30 Marks, or take the one from the chest in the Cutting). Corwin can Delay the Porter, and Wren's Hurry can move a friend up the order after a Check Tickets. Save your Tonics for the turn after Heavy Trunk, and carry an Oil Can (10 Marks) to cure Rusting after the Whistle. Rest at the save clock before the fight. If it feels hard, train a few extra fights on the road first, since the Rust returns after a rest.

    What the chapter leaves you with

    • The Station Escapement is back, the clock runs, and the first note from "S." is in your hands.
    • Corwin seems to have an idea who wrote the note.
    • The Hopfields Post: three letters from Aunt Agnes, and the path north.

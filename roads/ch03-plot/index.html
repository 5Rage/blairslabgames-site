    Chapter 3 plot and boss - The Toll Clock

    Two banks blaming each other, a courier from the held city, a night on the Belcourt wall and the Tollkeeper.

    Updated 11 November 2026

    Spoilers ahead.This page discusses story events and reveals. Come back after you have played if you want to find things out yourself.
    On this page
    1. What happens
    2. Party and moth
    3. Boss: The Tollkeeper
    4. What the chapter leaves you with

    Warning This page gives away the plot and the boss of this chapter.

    Place guide: Tollbridge and Belcourt. Previous: Chapter 2 plot and boss. Next: Chapter 4 plot and boss.

    What happens

    The Kittiwake ties up at Tollbridge and Bett says she is staying put until the town is sorted. The toll gates are shut, and the toll clock above them has stopped at twelve minutes past six again. People from both banks are frozen on the bridge, and from the rooftops Wren sees a city to the north standing perfectly still in the rain. "Is that the city? I thought it was a story," she says. Corwin tells her it is real, but nobody goes in. The two councils are no help: Councillor Pell insists it is the Lower Bank's clock and the Lower Bank's fault, while the notice on the other hall says the exact opposite.

    At the post office Wren meets Senn, a courier from the city, sorting thirty years of dead letters. He came out across the edge with a letter for the belfry at Belcourt and the road has been stuck ever since. He turns over a yellowed envelope addressed only "A., Hollowmere", with no street and no surname. Hollowmere, he says, is under a lake now. Wren cannot let it lie: somebody wrote it and somebody should get it, so she promises to find A. Senn gives her the letter, calls her "postie", and offers to walk with the party to Belcourt. Postmaster Oakes, who knows Aunt Agnes, offers Wren the courier's exam: four parcels, two for each bank.

    The road climbs to Belcourt, last town before the city, where the Rust comes up the hill every night and every night the town holds the wall. Senn greets Hester Bell like an old friend, and she asks the travellers to help hold the wall tonight. Three waves break against it and the party holds them all. Brother Anselm accepts the belfry letter, and Senn decides to stay at Belcourt and carry the town's post. Hester's grandson Pip Tallow, who watched the fight from the steeple, joins the party when he hears about the stopped toll clock, and Hester asks Wren to bring him back in one piece.

    Back at Tollbridge the way to the gates is open. Behind them a toll booth unfolds on iron legs, gate-bar arms swinging and a coin slot for a mouth, demanding the exact fare. Bett says she will give it exact change. When the Tollkeeper is beaten it folds down into an ordinary booth. Corwin finds the toll clock jammed with coins, as if someone fed it a fortune to make it stop. The moth circles the clock, the gates swing open, and people from both banks bump into each other and begin apologising. Behind the clock is a fourth note: "A bridge only joins two places that will one day forget each other. Let it rest. - S."

    Wren turns on Corwin, sure he knows who is writing these. He thinks he does, hopes he is wrong, and promises to tell her everything when they reach Hollowmere. That worries him, because Hollowmere is where Wren's dead letter is addressed. Pell and Marrow agree it was the travellers' doing, for the bridge is open. Pip heads home to the steeple, promising to hear it if you ever shout from somewhere high. Deliver the parcels and Oakes pins the Courier's Badge on Wren.

    Party and moth

    • Joins (guests): Senn at the Tollbridge post office, quick and slippery, with Pickpocket to lift things from the Rust and Shortcut to get back in at once. Pip Tallow at Belcourt, whose Long Shot never misses, even fliers, and whose Flare hits every foe with Spark.
    • Leaves: Senn stays at Belcourt after Brother Anselm accepts the belfry letter. If you miss that scene he turns back at the Tollbridge east gate. Pip leaves at the east gate to go home. They are guests only for this chapter.
    • Moth Light: none new.
    • Only four fight at once. Change the line-up in Menu: Party. Everyone still earns XP.

    Boss: The Tollkeeper

    The bridge's great toll machine. It is a clear step up from the Thresher. These are base figures, and the game tunes bosses higher.
    HP 2600
    Attack 46
    Defence 17
    Mind 41
    Speed 13
    XP / Marks 520 / 300
    Weak to Spark
    Resists Tide
    Drops Wind Draught (always)

    Moves

    • Toll Bar: an ordinary hit on one friend.
    • Exact Change: a magic attack on the whole party, 60% chance to leave each friend Slowed for 3 turns.
    • No Entry: a light hit with an 80% chance to leave the target Seized for 1 turn.
    • Wind the Gate: no damage; it hurries its own turns.
    • Gate Slam: BIG, one very heavy hit. Glow warns you a turn early.
    • Rush Hour: BIG, damages the whole party. Glow warns you a turn early.

    Pattern. Toll Bar, Exact Change, Gate Slam, Toll Bar, No Entry, Wind the Gate, Rush Hour, Toll Bar, Exact Change, Gate Slam, No Entry, Toll Bar, Rush Hour, then back to the start. A Gate Slam and a Rush Hour each come twice per cycle.

    Advice. Lead with Spark: Corwin's Arc, Spark Jars (30 Marks), and Pip's Flare. If Wren and Corwin's Bond has reached level 2, their Charged Delivery hits every foe with Spark. Skip Tide, since it resists it. Bett's Undertow and Brine Flasks are wasted here. Bett's Drop Anchor pulls Gate Slam and Toll Bar onto her. Heal with Hop Salve after Rush Hour. Oil Cans cure Seized, and Smelling Salts are worth carrying. Pip is still with you for this fight. If you want a break, rest and train on Tollbridge's Rust first, since they return after a rest.

    What the chapter leaves you with

    • The open toll gates, a fourth note from "S.", and the Courier's Badge if you finish the exam.
    • A Dead Letter addressed to "A., Hollowmere", and Corwin's promise to explain when you reach it.
    • The miners' road east to Lowdelve.
